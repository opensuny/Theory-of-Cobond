1. A military stalemate has emerged: Ukraine is unable to launch a counterattack to regain all its territories, and Russia also finds it difficult to completely defeat Ukraine. Both sides have reached a dynamic balance on the battlefield, and neither side can achieve its maximum goal through military means.
2. The possibility of changes in the war form: If Russia defeats the regular forces of Ukraine, it will also be trapped in a guerrilla war similar to those in Afghanistan and Chechnya. Modern technology has made guerrilla warfare less costly and more lethal, and Russia's population structure and economic situation determine that it cannot sustain such a huge consumption for a long time.
3. External intervention will continue and diversify: In addition to the public military assistance from the United States and Europe, other countries will also intervene with a "weapons testing ground" mentality, providing more advanced equipment to the Ukrainian guerrillas. Russia is not facing an isolated Ukraine, but rather a de facto "global weapons recipient".
4. A ceasefire is a no-go zone for both sides: Russia has paid a heavy price for this. If the final outcome is merely an "unfinished goal", then the political elites in Russia will face severe challenges. If Ukraine accepts a ceasefire agreement that leads to territorial loss, its political elites will also be swallowed up by the tide of nationalism. The political elites have already been hijacked by words and public opinion, and neither side dares to propose a ceasefire suggestion voluntarily.
5. Conclusion: War will be a long-term consumption, and it cannot be resolved in the short term.
