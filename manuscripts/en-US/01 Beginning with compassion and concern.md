When the cold wind from the Eastern European plain swept the remaining snow and passed along both banks of the Dnieper River, the flames of war were still burning on this land of the same origin, black and fertile. Road signs lay fallen in the already withered grass and ruins, stubbornly pointing to the names that no longer existed. Suddenly, the roar of artillery sounded, and flocks of crows suddenly took to the sky, emitting chaotic cries. Countless black wings flapped the air, darkly circling back and forth in the gray sky. Smoke spread with the wind, mingling with the smell of soil and burnt residue, spreading out across the plain.

A young Ukrainian mother, cradling her infant in swaddling clothes, walked through the thin layer of snow that had yet to melt in early spring to her husband’s gravestone. A cold wind swept across the open field, ruffling her thin headscarf. The child was too young to understand what a final farewell meant. Innocently, he stretched out his tiny hands—slightly reddened by the cold—and happily touched the photograph of his father on the gravestone, babbling with laughter, believing it to be a loved one who would always watch over him.
The mother stood silently; her tears had long since been blown dry by the cold wind. She gazed at her child’s innocent face and at the young face on the headstone, her eyes filled with boundless bewilderment, excruciating pain, and silent despair. She had lost her beloved husband; the child had lost a father he had never truly embraced. On this black soil—once sun-drenched and fertile with wheat—all that remained now was the acrid smell of gunpowder, a mother and her orphaned child, and sorrow stretching as far as the eye could see.

On the other side of the Don River, on the same fertile Russian soil, an elderly mother stood guard over an empty cottage. Outside, the cold wind blew from Siberia. Inside, the fire in the stove toasted the lepyopolsky bread to a faint aroma. Everything remained as it was when her son left home - his favorite black lepyopolsky bread on the table, the old tea cups he used from childhood on the bedside, and the simple tapestry passed down from his ancestors on the wall.
But she knew that the cheerful young man who would call out to her as "Mom", who would chop firewood for her, and who would give her flowers on festivals - that bright and lively boy - would never come in through the door again. She touched her son's photo over and over, without crying or shouting, only a kind of emptiness and silence. She received medals and honors, but lost the only light in her life. On this land renowned for its resilience, the rest of her life was filled only with endless waiting and disappointment.

In a village on the edge of the Maasai Mara grassland in Kenya, an elderly woman sat on the parched ground, feeding her hungry grandson the freshly baked bread. She might not know that the grain in her hands originated from the black soil of Eastern Europe, which was shrouded in smoke and war from afar. She also didn't know that on this vast African continent, there were hundreds of millions of people like her, whose livelihoods were tightly tied to distant countries at war - nearly half of the wheat came from there.
On one side, there are the wheat fields and sorrow of Ukraine; on the other side, there are the snowstorms and watchfulness of Russia. And these are all connected to the survival of hundreds of millions of people on the distant African continent.
The same Eastern European plain, the same deep black soil, yet they mutually harmed each other in the midst of gunfire, reducing countless families to ashes.

In the summer of 2026, on a typical afternoon, a line of words appeared on my phone screen, leaving me stunned: The Russia-Ukraine War had been ongoing for the fifth year. It was estimated that the cumulative casualties on both sides might have exceeded 2 million, and millions more people had been displaced from their homes.

These were not figures from history books; they were the remnants of 2 million lives that I, a person of my generation, was breathing in and witnessing - these precious lives could have been bathed in the sunshine with their families and displayed bright smiles. A sense of absurdity enveloped me along with a chill - humanity has connected the world village, cracked the genes, used AI to control robots, landed on the moon, visited Mars, and claimed to be civilized and advanced, yet still destroying each other in the most primitive way.

At this moment, a question that even a child could pose hit me hard: Why? Why has human civilization evolved for thousands of years and still cannot solve the problem of "how to live peacefully together"? This book is a long and arduous journey, attempting to find the ultimate answer, that began with the shock and questioning of that afternoon - this is also my, as an Earth citizen, compassion and concern for the common fate of humanity.

This question, like a seed, grew wildly in my heart. I began to re-examine the path that humanity has taken. We conquered the sky and the deep sea, but we couldn't conquer our inner greed; we built complex civilizations within our own countries, but we retained the most primitive jungle barriers between nations. I tried to find the answers in history books, but I found that the answers were not in the past, but right beside us - even on the ground beneath our feet.

**1. The Association of Ants and Humans**
By chance, I gazed at an ant nest for a long time, and what I saw was a perfect miniature society: each ant moved freely and in an orderly manner; they shared the temperature of the nest and the reserves of food; when facing enemies, they didn't hesitate to assemble and fight for the survival of the collective. Within the group, there was no confrontation or exploitation, and no unnecessary internal strife. This shocked me. Isn't this exactly what humans long for - unity, mutual assistance, and safety? The ants achieved this through instinct.

However, when I raised my head and looked at another ant nest nearby, the scene changed abruptly. Once the two groups of ants met, they immediately engaged in a fierce battle, for territory, for food, without hesitation, destroying each other. My heart sank suddenly. This scene is so familiar!
We, who consider ourselves highly intelligent humans, aren't this exactly the case? Within a country, we learn to coexist and help each other, establish laws and ethics; but between countries, we are like ants, continuing to plunder resources, ideas and power for countless millions of years, while retaining the habits of the insect race. Is this the stagnation of civilized evolution?

**2. From Shock to Inquiry: The Emergence of Basic Axioms**
This sense of absurdity compelled me to embark on a quest for answers. I cast aside the complex terminology and returned to the most fundamental starting point of life. I discovered that in the vast universe, life moves against entropy, only for one purpose - to live and to continue. This is the survival instinct engraved in our genes.
Then, what does life rely on for survival? It relies on "form (The appearance or manifestation of the existence of things)". From single cells aggregating into multi-cells, from wolves forming packs, from humans establishing families and nations, life continuously creates more advanced organizational forms. And whether these forms can survive depends on an ultimate criterion - whether it effectively serves the individuals that constitute it.

Looking at history, any form that can make the majority of members live better and with more dignity (such as the early, enlightened golden age of Roman law) will thrive; any form that becomes a privilege for a few and harms the masses (such as the decadent empire) will eventually collapse. Service effectiveness is the measuring instrument of the rise and fall of civilizations.

Then I reflected and discovered: All forms are constantly changing. Isn't there a perfect form? I found the third axiom - the imperfect state is the foundation and driving force of the existence and evolution of things.

This is a seemingly paradoxical yet governing law that governs everything: If life is perfect, it would not need to exist or continue - because perfection means nothingness, nothingness means constancy, constancy means stillness, and stillness means death. The reason why life is life is precisely because it is imperfect; it needs to make up for the deficiencies, so it needs to exist; it needs to resist entropy increase, so it needs to continue; it needs to surpass the present, so it needs to evolve. This axiom, when incorporated into international politics, reveals a force that has shaken the world throughout history:
① It pronounced the death sentence for the ultimate perfect solution. Any scheme that claims to "once and for all end war" and any conception that attempts to establish a "perfect world government" violates the Axiom of the Perfect Paradox - they pursue an impossible perfection, and thus are bound to collapse in reality. The predicament of the EU model, the failure of the UN system, and the alternation of successive hegemonic orders are all bloodstained confirmations of this axiom.
② It reveals the wisdom of imperfect coexistence. Since perfection is unattainable, the survival of civilization does not depend on whether all contradictions can be eliminated, but on whether it can coexist and evolve in the imperfect state. The failure of the exclusive security path lies in its attempt to achieve its own "perfect security" by eliminating "others" - this itself is a violation of the Axiom of the Perfect Paradox.
③ It indicates the structural philosophy of the Cobond Theory. The four pillars of "sovereign independence, military interlocking, social integration, and civilization perpetual prosperity covenant" do not claim to be "perfect solutions" from the very beginning - they consider themselves imperfect, iterative, and need periodic hedging. Therefore, they can become truly sustainable solutions. Cobond is not the ultimate perfect solution, but an unfinished civilization project - it offsets contradictions in each cycle and approaches a higher level of imperfection in each evolution, thereby obtaining eternal vitality.

The Axiom of the Perfect Paradox is the fundamental mark that distinguishes the Cobond Theory from all utopian schemes. Utopianism pursues perfection, and thus inevitably ends in tyranny; Cobond acknowledges imperfection, and thus its form needs to be continuously optimized.

**III. Cobond: Breaking Through the Civilizational Dilemma**
Cobond (in a broad sense): A symbiotic relationship form composed of multiple independent entities, where shared core functions create a safe and common destiny.
Cobond (in the narrow sense - in the field of international politics): A symbiotic relationship form composed of multiple independent entities, with shared interlocking in the security domain and independent domains in other areas.

Given this, the current predicament of humanity is clearly evident. We are skilled at constructing "internal Cobonds" (countries), but are poor at handling "external relations" (international affairs). We are trapped by the barriers between "us" and "them" and have fallen into a zero-sum game-style dead loop - arms races, debt traps, proxy wars... Our most intelligent minds are busy designing more efficient schemes for mutual destruction.

Human civilization must have a breakthrough. This is not a moral appeal, but a structural reconstruction. Based on three fundamental axioms, Cobond was proposed. Through shared interlocking in the military domain, economic incentives and physical possibilities for war are eliminated; through autonomy in other domains, each retains its independence and the diversity of civilization; through distributed architecture, the system can be infinitely expanded and achieve super resilience. Looking at all global governance and regional integration theories since modern times, whether it is confederations, federations, alliances, or collective security mechanisms, they have not broken away from the inherent framework of "each country's military being independent and mutually deterrence-balancing", and are still trapped in the zero-sum game and Liro confrontation cycle.

The breakthrough originality of "Cobond Theory" is concentrated in the design of the military interlocking architecture: through cross-border military mixed formation and structural transformation with mutual embedding of rights and responsibilities, the war is eliminated from the physical mechanism.
Based on the three axioms as the foundation, supported by the four pillars of sovereign independence, military interlocking, social integration, and civilization perpetual prosperity covenant, and with the entire set of human civilization engineering as the implementation methodology, they jointly form a complete plan of a self-consistent, practical, and sustainable new form of human civilization.

**IV. Beyond Earth: Survival Laws on Cosmic Scales**
During the process of writing this book, my thoughts did not remain confined to the biosphere of Earth. When I looked up at the stars, a question emerged: If there are countless civilizations in the vast universe, do they also face the same challenges?

The first axiom states: The first requirement of life is survival. However, in the cold vacuum, radiation, and asteroid belts of the universe, the survival of individuals is how fragile. Any civilization attempting to traverse the starry sea, if its interior is filled with division and internal strife, if it cannot use its limited resources to jointly resist the risks of the universe, but instead consumes them on internal mutual deterrence, then it is likely to quietly die out during its infancy. This is the projection of the "Great Filter" theory at the level of civilization sociology. Perhaps the ultimate assessment by the universe for all intelligent life is not whether it can fly out of its mother planet, but whether it can, before acquiring destructive power, first learn to construct a stable symbiotic form that can control this power.

From this perspective, the Cobond has acquired universality beyond Earth. It is not only a solution to the disputes of human countries, but also possibly the cosmic civilization engineering that intelligent life in the universe must follow for long-term survival.

Within a star system, the Cobond may manifest as cross-planetary resource sharing and risk mutual defense. Between interstellar civilizations, it may evolve into a "cosmic distributed architecture" based on energy conservation and information transparency. Our exploration of the Cobond on Earth is actually a necessary first thought exercise for humanity's possible interstellar future. If we cannot peacefully share this blue marble beneath our feet, how can we talk about the starry sea?
